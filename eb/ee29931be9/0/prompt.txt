### 3. Layout stability — verified, clean. Measured, not eyeballed.

`PerformanceObserver({type:'layout-shift'})` over a full-document scroll at all three viewports, repeated under injected optimiser latency of 150/500/1000/2343 ms: **CLS = 0.000 in every run.** No shift entry was ever attributed to a card. ### 2. The placeholder — this is the most serious finding, and it is worse than you framed it.

`bg-zinc-100` (#f4f4f5) against the band it sits in, computed in-page:

| band | band bg | contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Backpacks, Hiking Footwear, Camping Stoves (`bg-zinc-100`) | #f4f4f5 | **1.000:1** |
| Hiking Clothing, Camping Tables, Sleeping Bags (`bg-inherit` → body) | #fafafa | **1.053:1** |

The `bg-inherit` bands do not resolve to white — they resolve to the body's #fafafa (zinc-50). So the placeholder is invisible in the alternating bands and all but invisible in the others. Nothing here reaches the 3:1 that a meaningful non-text boundary needs.

`shots/prehydration-very-tall-cpu4.png` is the evidence: the Backpacks band is a 400px-tall void with three product names floating at the bottom and nothing above them. It does not read as loading. It reads as a category whose products failed.

And there is a second empty state you may not have noticed, which is the one users actually see most of the time. When the observer fires, the placeholder `div` unmounts and is replaced by `next/image`'s `&lt;img&gt;`, which carries `style="color:transparent"` and **no background at all**. Since the observer fires 600px before entry, by the time a card is on screen it is almost always this transparent `&lt;img&gt;`, not the grey div. So the grey box is doing essentially no work in the visible region — the loading state a scrolling user sees is a fully transparent gap, contrast 1.000:1 against every band. `shots/desktop-03-placeholder-zincband.png` and `shots/desktop-04-fling-immediate.png` are both this state.

Suggested fix: put the tone on the *image* as well as the placeholder, so the reserved box is visible in both states — `next/image`'s `placeholder="empty"` leaves it bare, so a `bg-zinc-200` (#e4e4e7, 1.9:1 vs the zinc band) or better `bg-zinc-300` (#d4d4d8, 2.9:1) on a wrapper that persists across the swap. A wrapper also removes the unmount/remount flicker. If you keep a bare tone, `zinc-200` is the minimum that reads as a box at all; `zinc-300` is the first step that approaches 3:1.

### 1. Pop-in — 600px is enough on a fast server and not enough on the server #230 was about.

The relationship is exact: the margin buys `600 / v` seconds of lead, so it covers latency `T` only while `T ≤ 600/v`. Measured on desktop, sweeping injected optimiser latency against scroll speed:

| per-image latency | scroll speed | cards grey on entry | worst gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 150 ms | 2500 px/s (fling) | 0 / 20 | 0 ms |
| 500 ms | 2500 px/s (fling) | 14 / 20 | 317 ms / 798 px |
| 1000 ms | 800 px/s (reading) | 14 / 20 | 283 ms / 221 px |
| 2343 ms (#230's measured median) | 800 px/s | 14 / 20 | 1633 ms / **1274 px** |

At the last row the user scrolls more than a full desktop viewport past a card before its image appears. On **phone** at that latency it is qualitatively worse: 14 of 20 cards **never decoded while they were on screen at all** — a 175px card plus caption is only on screen for ~1.34s at 800 px/s, less than the fetch, so the visitor scrolls the entire page and sees images only in the first category. That is the run behind `shots/phone-04-fling-immediate.png`.

Unthrottled against a warm optimiser on this 24-core host, per-image latency is 10–95ms and the gap is **0ms at every viewport at 800 px/s and at a 2500 px/s fling**. So on this machine 600px is fine. Your CI box is not this machine: cold optimisation there measured 2343ms at 18-way concurrency, and the fix cuts concurrency to 6, which on this host is worth roughly 2× (median 25ms → 13ms) — so expect something in the high hundreds of ms to ~1.2s on a 2-core runner, not 2343ms.

**The margin I would use: 1200px**, and I would express it as a value derived from the viewport rather than a constant. The cost is bounded and I measured it:

| rootMargin | phone reqs | tablet reqs | desktop reqs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 600 (current) | 3 | 6 | 6 |
| 900 | 3 | 6 | 6 |
| **1200** | **5** | **9** | **9** |
| 1800 | 6 | 12 | 12 |
| 3900 (Chrome's own) | 12 | 20 | 20 |

1200px keeps you at half of Chrome's behaviour and well under the spec's `MAX_INITIAL_REQUESTS = 8`… except at tablet and desktop, where 9 would **fail your own new test**. Note that: `apps/web/e2e/image-deferral.spec.ts:48` pins the ceiling at 8, which quietly forbids any margin above ~900px. If you raise the margin you must raise that bound, and the comment there ("a small multiple of what is on screen") supports 9 fine. 1200px covers 512 px/s at 2343ms latency and 2400 px/s at 500ms — it does not cover a hard fling on a slow server, and no margin short of ~7000px does, which is why the visible-placeholder fix above matters more than the margin does.

### 4. The eager cutoff — no, `i === 0` is too tight, and the reason is hydration, not geometry.

The observer does fire immediately for anything within 600px of the fold, so at tall viewports the second and third categories are *requested* at first paint. The problem is *when*. Deferred images cannot be requested until the client component hydrates:

| CPU throttle | first eager image request | first deferred image request | lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1× | 159 ms | 396 ms | +237 ms |
| 4× | 320 ms | 1727 ms | **+1.4 s** |
| 6× | 388 ms | 2357 ms | **+2.0 s** |

At 1440×2000, **9 cards are above the fold and only 3 are eager**; the other 6 are grey placeholders on screen for that entire window before the request even starts, then plus the optimisation. At 1024×1366 and 1440×1440, 6 are above the fold and 3 are eager. `shots/prehydration-very-tall-cpu4.png` is exactly this. Landscape phone 844×390 is fine — zero cards are above the fold there, the hero fills it.

Suggested fix: make the cutoff cover the plausible first screen rather than the first category — `eager={i === 0 || i === 1}` costs 3 extra server-rendered images at every viewport and removes the pre-hydration hole at every viewport I tested up to 1440×2000. That puts first-paint requests at 6/9/9, the same as the 1200px margin would, so the two changes overlap rather than compound.

## Judgement: "observe, never fire" as the polyfill default

**I agree with the choice.** The reasoning that would make me push back — "a component that genuinely needs the observer to fire silently renders its empty state and nobody notices" — doesn't hold here, because the failure mode is loud, not silent: any test that expects the *visible* state (an `&lt;img&gt;` with a `/_next/image` src, a queryable `role="img"`, etc.) gets nothing in the DOM and its assertion fails outright. That's the opposite of a trap. The actual trap would be the reverse default — firing on construction — which is the one that makes deferral itself untestable and would let a regression that deleted the deferral entirely (i.e., un-fixing #230) pass every unit test silently, since "always visible" would be indistinguishable from "correctly deferred, then arrived." `listing-image.test.tsx` already demonstrates the correct escape hatch (`vi.stubGlobal` + `afterEach: vi.unstubAllGlobals()` to drive `arrive()` locally), so it's a discoverable pattern, not a one-off.

One real but different edge worth watching, not a defect in this choice: the risk this default does create is a *vacuous pass*, not a silent one — a future test that walks a full rendered page asserting something generic across "every image" (e.g., "every image has valid dimensions") would see zero images for any deferred card and pass trivially, because nothing rendered, not because the property held. Neither `page.test.tsx` nor `listing-image.test.tsx` falls into this today; flagging it only as the shape of trap to watch for as broader/whole-page unit tests are added later — any such test needs to either render eagerly or drive the observer itself, same as `listing-image.test.tsx` already does.
